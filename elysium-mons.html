Elysium Mons

A large volcano in the Elysium Planitia region of Mars

Summit caldera of Elysium Mons.
Lava flows on the flanks of the volcano.

Key Facts

  • Height: 14 km (8.7 miles) above the surrounding plains
  • Diameter: 240 km (149 miles)
  • Summit caldera: 14 km (8.7 miles) in diameter
  • Location: Elysium Planitia region, at 25.0°N 147.2°E
  • Age: Estimated 3-4 billion years old
  • Type: Shield volcano

Elysium Mons is one of the main volcanoes in the Elysium volcanic province, which is the second largest volcanic region on Mars after the Tharsis region. The volcano is surrounded by an extensive lava plain and is part of a complex that includes two other volcanoes: Hecates Tholus and Albor Tholus.

Unlike the Tharsis volcanoes, Elysium Mons has steeper slopes, suggesting a different composition of lava or eruption style. The volcano's flanks are marked by numerous channels that may have been formed by flowing lava or water from melted subsurface ice.
