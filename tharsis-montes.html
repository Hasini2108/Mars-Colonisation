Tharsis Montes

A group of three large shield volcanoes on Mars

Arsia Mons, the southernmost of the three volcanoes.
Pavonis Mons, the central volcano of the three.
Ascraeus Mons, the northernmost and tallest of the three volcanoes.

The Three Volcanoes

Arsia Mons

  • Height: 17.7 km (11 miles)
  • Diameter: 435 km (270 miles)
  • Location: Southernmost volcano

Pavonis Mons

  • Height: 14 km (8.7 miles)
  • Diameter: 375 km (233 miles)
  • Location: Central volcano

Ascraeus Mons

  • Height: 18 km (11.2 miles)
  • Diameter: 460 km (286 miles)
  • Location: Northernmost volcano

Key Facts

  • The three volcanoes are aligned along the Tharsis Ridge
  • They are shield volcanoes, formed by many layers of fluid lava flows
  • They are located on the Tharsis Bulge, a massive volcanic plateau
  • The volcanoes are spaced about 700 km (435 miles) apart
  • They are aligned in a northeast-southwest direction
  • Last eruption: Estimated 100-300 million years ago

The Tharsis Montes are part of the larger Tharsis region, a vast volcanic plateau that contains several other volcanoes including Olympus Mons. The alignment of these three massive volcanoes is thought to be the result of plate movement over a mantle hotspot or a zone of weakness in the Martian crust.
