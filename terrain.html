Mars Terrain

The Red Planet's Landscape

Mars has some of the most dramatic landscapes in our solar system, including the largest volcano and the deepest canyon. The planet's surface is characterized by impact craters, valleys, dunes, and polar ice caps.

The distinctive red color of Mars comes from iron oxide (rust) on its surface. The planet experiences dust storms that can cover the entire planet and last for months.

Active Volcanoes on Mars

Olympus Mons

The largest volcano in the solar system, standing at 22 km (13.6 miles) high and spanning 600 km (372 miles) in diameter.

Status

Dormant

Last Eruption

Estimated 2-25 million years ago

Tharsis Montes

A group of three large shield volcanoes: Arsia Mons, Pavonis Mons, and Ascraeus Mons, aligned along the Tharsis Ridge.

Status

Dormant

Last Eruption

Estimated 100-300 million years ago

Elysium Mons

A large volcano in the Elysium Planitia region, rising 14 km (8.7 miles) above the surrounding plains.

Status

Dormant

Last Eruption

Estimated 3-4 billion years ago

Other Notable Features

Valles Marineris

Often called the "Grand Canyon of Mars," Valles Marineris is a system of canyons that runs along the Martian equator. It stretches for nearly 4,000 km (2,500 miles) and reaches depths of up to 7 km (4.3 miles).

Polar Ice Caps

Mars has two permanent polar ice caps composed of water ice and carbon dioxide ice. These caps grow and shrink with the Martian seasons, and studying them helps scientists understand Mars' climate history.
