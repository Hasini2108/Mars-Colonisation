MARS MISSIONS

NASA’s various Mars missions, ranging from rovers like Opportunity and Curiosity to orbiters like MAVEN
and Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter, are driven by a singular goal: to unravel the mysteries of the Red Planet.
These missions aim to understand Mars' history, its potential to support life, and the possibility of human
exploration in the future. By sending rovers to explore the surface, landers to study the planet's interior, and
orbiters to map its atmosphere, NASA seeks to gather crucial data on Mars' geology, climate, and habitability.
The Viking missions in the 1970s set the stage for these investigations by focusing on the search for life and
the study of Mars' soil and atmosphere. More recent missions, such as Perseverance, aim to investigate
ancient signs of life and collect samples that may one day be returned to Earth. Additionally, missions like the
Mars 2020 Orbiter support data relay systems to maintain communication with the Martian surface.
With the ultimate intention of answering whether Mars once hosted life and whether humans could one day
live there, NASA’s commitment to exploring Mars continues to push the boundaries of space exploration,
paving the way for future missions like the Mars Sample Return and human missions to the planet.
Through these missions, NASA is setting the foundation for humanity's next steps in the cosmos.

Curiosity Rover Investigate Mars' climate, geology, and potential habitability. November 26, 2011 August 6, 2012 Confirmed presence of ancient water, discovered organic molecules, and methane spikes.
Perseverance Rover Search for signs of ancient life, collect samples for future return missions, and test new technologies. July 30, 2020 February 18, 2021 Exploring Jezero Crater, investigating ancient river delta, and testing the first helicopter on Mars (Ingenuity).
InSight Lander Study the interior of Mars, its seismic activity, and temperature. May 5, 2018 November 26, 2018 Detected Marsquakes, studied heat flow, and analyzed the planet's internal structure.
